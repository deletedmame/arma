Unit 1: Introduction to Biology

1.1 Definition of Biology

Biology is the branch of science that deals with the study of living organisms and their vital processes. It explores how life works, from the molecular level in cells to the complex interactions in entire ecosystems.

1.2 Why do we study Biology?

Understanding biology is crucial for medical advancement, environmental conservation, agriculture, and understanding the impact of humans on the planet.

1.3 The Scientific Method

Biologists use a structured process to solve problems and answer questions:

  • Observation: Identifying a pattern or problem.
  • Hypothesis: Proposing a testable explanation.
  • Experiment: Testing the hypothesis under controlled variables.
  • Data Analysis: Collecting and interpreting results.
  • Conclusion: Determining if the results support the hypothesis.

1.4 Tools of a Biologist

Biological study requires specialized equipment for observation and data collection.

1.4.1 Laboratory Tools

Common tools include beakers, test tubes, petri dishes, pipettes, and balances used to measure and contain biological samples.

1.4.2 Field Tools

These include quadrats, transect lines, GPS devices, and collection nets used to study organisms in their natural habitats.

1.5 The Light Microscope

The light microscope uses visible light and lenses to magnify small images, making it possible to see cells and tissues.

1.5.1 Parts and Functions

  • Eyepiece (Ocular): The lens you look through.
  • Objective Lenses: Lenses with different magnifications.
  • Coarse Adjustment: Moves the stage for rough focusing.
  • Fine Adjustment: Used for precise sharpening of the image.

1.5.2 Handling and Usage

Always carry a microscope by the arm and the base. Use lens paper to clean the glass and always start with the lowest power objective lens.

1.6 General Laboratory Safety Rules

Safety is the top priority. Always wear personal protective equipment (PPE), never taste chemicals, and wash your hands thoroughly after every experiment.
