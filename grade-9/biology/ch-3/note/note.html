Unit 3: Cells

Based on textbook pages 49-78

3.1 What is a Cell?

The cell is the smallest and basic unit of living things. All living things are composed of cells.

  • Unicellular organisms: Consist of a single cell (e.g., Paramecium, bacteria).
  • Multicellular organisms: Consist of many specialized cells working cooperatively (e.g., plants, humans).

Fun Fact: An ostrich egg is a single cell weighing about 1.36 kg!

3.2 Cell Theory

Developed by Schleiden, Schwann, and Virchow, the Cell Theory states:

  1. Living organisms are composed of one or more cells.
  2. Cells are the smallest unit of life.
  3. Cells arise only by the division of a pre-existing cell (Omnis cellula e cellula).

Robert Hooke (1665) first observed cells in cork. Anton van Leeuwenhoek observed living "animalcules" (bacteria/protists).

3.3 Cell Structure and Function

Organelles

  • Nucleus: Control center, contains DNA (chromosomes). Site of ribosome synthesis (nucleolus).
  • Cytoplasm: Fluid-filled region (70-80% water) where organelles are suspended and chemical reactions occur.
  • Cell Membrane: Selectively permeable barrier controlling entry/exit of substances.
  • Cell Wall: Rigid outer layer in plants, fungi, and bacteria (made of cellulose in plants). Absent in animal cells.
  • Mitochondria: "Powerhouse" of the cell; site of cellular respiration (ATP production).
  • Ribosomes: Site of protein synthesis. Found freely in cytoplasm or attached to Rough ER.
  • Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER):
    • Rough ER: Studded with ribosomes; synthesizes and transports proteins.
    • Smooth ER: No ribosomes; synthesizes lipids and detoxifies chemicals.
  • Golgi Apparatus: Modifies, sorts, and packages proteins and lipids into vesicles for transport.
  • Lysosomes: Contain digestive enzymes to break down waste, bacteria, and damaged organelles.
  • Chloroplasts: Plant cells only. Site of photosynthesis; contains chlorophyll pigment.
  • Vacuoles: Storage sacs. Large central vacuole in plants (stores water, pigments, wastes). Small in animals.
  • Centrioles: Animal cells only. Involved in cell division (organizing microtubules).
  • Cilia & Flagella: Hair-like structures for locomotion or moving fluids.

3.4 Types of Cells

Prokaryotic Cells

  • No nucleus or membrane-bound organelles. DNA is circular and free in the cytoplasm. Smaller and simpler (e.g., Bacteria and Archaea).

Eukaryotic Cells

  • Contain a "true nucleus" and membrane-bound organelles. Larger and more complex (e.g., Plants, Animals, Fungi, Protists).

3.5 Animal and Plant Cells

Similarities: Nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane, mitochondria, ER, Golgi, ribosomes.

Differences (Plant vs. Animal):

  • ✅ Cell Wall (Cellulose) vs. ❌ No Cell Wall
  • ✅ Chloroplasts vs. ❌ No Chloroplasts
  • ✅ Large Central Vacuole vs. Small/Temporary Vacuoles
  • ❌ No Centrioles vs. ✅ Centrioles

3.6 Observing Cells Under a Microscope

Total Magnification = Eyepiece Lens × Objective Lens (e.g., 10x × 40x = 400x).

Staining: Iodine solution for plant cells (onion epidermis) to see the nucleus and starch. Methylene blue for animal cells (cheek cells) to see the nucleus clearly.

3.7 The Cell and Its Environment

The cell membrane is selectively permeable.

Passive Transport (No Energy Required)

  • Diffusion: Movement of molecules from high to low concentration (e.g., oxygen entering blood).
  • Osmosis: Movement of water across a semi-permeable membrane from high water potential (dilute) to low water potential (concentrated).

Osmosis in Cells

  • Hypotonic Solution: Water enters cell. Animal cells burst (hemolysis); Plant cells become turgid (firm).
  • Hypertonic Solution: Water leaves cell. Animal cells shrink; Plant cells undergo plasmolysis (cell membrane pulls away from wall).
  • Isotonic Solution: No net movement; cell stable.

Active Transport

  • Movement of molecules against the concentration gradient (low to high). Requires energy (ATP). Example: Uptake of ions by root hairs.

3.8 Levels of Biological Organization

Life is organized in a hierarchy:

  1. Atoms → Molecules → Organelles
  2. Cells (Basic unit of life)
  3. Tissues: Group of similar cells performing a function (e.g., muscle tissue, xylem).
  4. Organs: Group of tissues working together (e.g., heart, leaf).
  5. Organ Systems: Group of organs working together (e.g., digestive system).
  6. Organism: Individual living thing.
