Unit 1: Introduction to Biology

Based on textbook pages 1-18

1.1 Definition of Biology

Biology is the branch of science that deals with the study of living organisms and their vital processes. The word comes from Greek: bios (life) and logos (study). It explores how life works, from the molecular level in cells to the complex interactions in entire ecosystems.

1.2 Why do we study Biology?

Understanding biology is crucial for:

  • Medical advancement - developing drugs and treatments
  • Environmental conservation - protecting ecosystems
  • Agriculture - improving crop yields and food production
  • Understanding human impact on the planet
  • Forensic science - solving crimes using DNA evidence

1.3 The Scientific Method

Biologists use a structured process to solve problems and answer questions:

  1. Observation: Identifying a pattern or problem
  2. Question: Formulating a specific question
  3. Hypothesis: Proposing a testable explanation
  4. Experiment: Testing under controlled variables
  5. Data Analysis: Collecting and interpreting results
  6. Conclusion: Determining if hypothesis is supported
  7. Communication: Sharing findings

1.4 Tools of a Biologist

1.4.1 Laboratory Tools

  • Beakers & Flasks: For mixing and heating solutions
  • Test Tubes: For small-scale reactions
  • Petri Dishes: For culturing microorganisms
  • Autoclave: For sterilizing equipment
  • Incubator: For maintaining optimal temperatures

1.4.2 Field Tools

  • Quadrats: For sampling plant populations
  • Transect lines: For studying distribution patterns
  • GPS devices: For location mapping
  • Collection nets: For capturing insects

1.5 The Light Microscope

1.5.1 Parts and Functions

  • Eyepiece (Ocular): 10x magnification
  • Objective Lenses: 4x, 10x, 40x, 100x
  • Coarse Adjustment: Rough focus
  • Fine Adjustment: Sharp focus
  • Stage: Holds the slide
  • Diaphragm: Controls light amount

1.5.2 Total Magnification

Total = Eyepiece × Objective (e.g., 10x × 40x = 400x)

1.5.3 Handling

  • Carry by arm and base
  • Start with lowest power (4x)
  • Use coarse then fine adjustment
  • Clean lenses with lens paper only

1.6 Laboratory Safety Rules

  • ✅ Follow instructions carefully
  • ✅ Wear PPE (goggles, lab coat, gloves)
  • ✅ Never eat or drink in lab
  • ✅ Never taste or sniff chemicals
  • ✅ Clean up after experiments
  • ✅ Wash hands thoroughly
