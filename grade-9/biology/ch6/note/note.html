Unit 6: Ecology

Based on textbook pages 142-165

6.1 Ecology

Ecology is the study of relationships of organisms to one another and to their environment.

6.1.2 Biotic and Abiotic Components

  • Abiotic Factors (Non-living): Energy (sunlight), Temperature, Water, Nutrients, Salinity, Wind, Soil pH.
  • Biotic Factors (Living): Predators, Herbivores, Parasites, Competitors, Pollinators.

6.1.3 Ecological Levels

  1. Organism: Individual living thing.
  2. Population: Group of interbreeding individuals of the same species in an area.
  3. Community: All populations of different species interacting in an area.
  4. Ecosystem: The community plus the abiotic environment.
  5. Biome: Large geographic area with similar climate and dominant life forms.
  6. Biosphere: Global sum of all ecosystems.

6.1.4 Ecosystems

A self-supporting unit comprising the community and its physical environment. Energy flows through, and nutrients cycle within.

  • Terrestrial: Forests, grasslands, deserts.
  • Aquatic: Freshwater (lakes, rivers, wetlands) and Marine (oceans, estuaries, coral reefs).

6.1.5 Biomes

Terrestrial Biomes

  • Tropical Rainforest: High rainfall, high biodiversity, evergreen broadleaf trees.
  • Desert: < 25 cm rain/year. Extreme temperatures. Adaptations: water storage (cacti), nocturnal animals.
  • Savanna: Tropical grassland with scattered trees. Warm year-round. Distinct wet/dry seasons. Supports large herbivores (zebras, giraffes) and predators (lions).
  • Temperate Grassland: Hot summers, cold winters. Fertile soil (prairies).
  • Boreal Forest (Taiga): Cold, coniferous trees (pine, spruce).
  • Temperate Broadleaf Forest: Deciduous trees that drop leaves in winter.
  • Tundra: Coldest biome. Permafrost. Low vegetation (mosses, lichens).

Aquatic Biomes

  • Lakes & Ponds: Standing freshwater. Zonation based on light penetration (photic vs. aphotic).
  • Wetlands: Soil saturated with water (marshes, swamps). High productivity; natural water filters.
  • Streams & Rivers: Flowing freshwater. Headwaters cool and clear; downstream warmer and murkier.
  • Estuaries: Transition zone between river and sea. Varying salinity. Highly productive nursery grounds.

6.1.6 Ecological Succession

  • Primary Succession: Colonization of barren land with no soil (e.g., new volcanic island). Pioneer species: Lichens, mosses.
  • Secondary Succession: Recolonization after a disturbance (e.g., fire, farming) where soil remains. Faster than primary.
  • Climax Community: Stable, mature community at the end of succession.

6.2 Ecological Relationships

  • Competition (-/-): Both species harmed vying for limited resources.
  • Predation (+/-): Predator kills and eats prey.
  • Herbivory (+/-): Herbivore eats parts of a plant.
  • Parasitism (+/-): Parasite benefits at host's expense (e.g., tapeworm).
  • Mutualism (+/+): Both species benefit (e.g., bees pollinating flowers, mycorrhizae).
  • Commensalism (+/0): One benefits, the other is unaffected (e.g., barnacles on a whale).
