Unit 4: Reproduction

Based on textbook pages 79-107

4.1 Introduction to Reproduction

Reproduction is the biological process by which new individual organisms ("offspring") are produced from their "parents". It is a fundamental feature of all known life.

4.2 Asexual Reproduction

Production of offspring without gametes (sex cells). Involves only one parent and produces genetically identical clones.

  • Advantages: No mate needed; rapid population growth; good traits passed on.
  • Disadvantages: No genetic variation; vulnerable to environmental changes/diseases.

4.3 Types of Asexual Reproduction

  • Fission: Organism splits into two (binary) or more (multiple) parts (e.g., bacteria, amoeba).
  • Fragmentation: Parent breaks into pieces, each regenerating into a new organism (e.g., Spirogyra, starfish).
  • Budding: New organism develops as an outgrowth (bud) from the parent (e.g., yeast, hydra).
  • Sporulation: Formation of spores that germinate into new individuals (e.g., fungi like Mucor).
  • Vegetative Propagation: New plants grow from vegetative parts (stems, roots, leaves). Examples: Runners (strawberry), Rhizomes (ginger), Tubers (potato), Bulbs (onion).
  • Parthenogenesis: Development of an unfertilized egg into an adult (e.g., male drones in honeybees, whiptail lizards).

4.4 Sexual Reproduction in Humans

Involves the fusion of male and female gametes (sperm and egg) to form a genetically unique zygote. Occurs through meiosis.

Fertilization: Union of sperm and egg nucleus.

4.5 Primary and Secondary Sexual Characteristics

  • Primary: Organs present at birth (penis, testes, ovaries, uterus).
  • Secondary: Develop during puberty (controlled by testosterone and estrogen).
    • Males: Deepening voice, facial/pubic hair, muscle growth.
    • Females: Breast development, widening hips, pubic hair, menstruation onset.

4.6 Male Reproductive Structures

  • Testes: Produce sperm and testosterone (housed in scrotum for temperature regulation).
  • Epididymis: Stores and matures sperm.
  • Vas Deferens: Transports sperm to urethra.
  • Accessory Glands: Seminal vesicles, prostate gland, bulbourethral glands produce seminal fluid (semen) to nourish and transport sperm.

4.7 Female Reproductive Structures

  • Ovaries: Produce eggs (ova) and hormones (estrogen, progesterone).
  • Oviducts (Fallopian Tubes): Site of fertilization; transports egg to uterus.
  • Uterus (Womb): Muscular organ where fetus develops.
  • Cervix: Neck of the uterus opening into the vagina.
  • Vagina: Birth canal and organ of intercourse.

4.8 The Menstrual Cycle

Approximately 28-day cycle preparing the uterus for pregnancy.

  • Follicular Phase: FSH stimulates follicle growth; estrogen thickens uterine lining.
  • Ovulation: LH surge triggers release of mature egg.
  • Luteal Phase: Corpus luteum secretes progesterone to maintain lining.
  • Menstruation: If no fertilization, lining sheds (period).

4.9 Fertilization and Pregnancy

  • Fertilization: Sperm penetrates egg in oviduct → forms Zygote.
  • Implantation: Embryo embeds in uterine wall.
  • Placenta & Umbilical Cord: Provides oxygen/nutrients from mother; removes waste.
  • Twins: Identical (one zygote splits) or Fraternal (two separate eggs fertilized).

4.10 Methods of Birth Control

  • Natural: Abstinence, rhythm method (tracking cycle).
  • Barrier: Condom (male/female), Diaphragm (blocks sperm; prevents STIs).
  • Hormonal: Pill, Implant, Injection (prevents ovulation).
  • Intrauterine Devices (IUD): Prevents implantation.
  • Surgical: Vasectomy (male), Tubal Ligation (female) – permanent sterilization.

4.11 Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs)

Infections spread primarily through sexual contact.

  • Bacterial: Chlamydia, Gonorrhea, Syphilis (Treatable with antibiotics).
  • Viral: HIV/AIDS, Genital Herpes (No cure, but manageable).
  • Protozoan: Trichomoniasis.

Prevention: Abstinence, mutual faithfulness, condom use.
